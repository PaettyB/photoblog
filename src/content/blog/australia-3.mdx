## April

If, after reading [the previous post](/blog/australia-2) about my exchange, you thought my travels were slowing down, you were wrong. They were just getting started. Following the surf camp, I spent a little over one week doing all assignments for uni and catching up on the lectures, which was not too hard. Then, it was already time to head to the airport to fly to New Zealand. It was a trip that we had only planned a few weeks in advance, where we rented a car for one week to do a road trip through the South Island, sleeping in a different hostel almost every night, while trying to cover as many highlights of the island as possible. It would turn out to be one of the best trips of my life.

With all of us being on a student budged, the trip started in the typical, cost-cutting manner: We took one of the last trains from Brisbane to the Gold Coast, from where our plane would depart, and spent the night at the beach before going to the airport for our 6am flight. It was another reminder that with the right kind of people anything can be a good time. Speaking of the right kind of people, our road trip crew was made up of Celina, Jessica, Vincent, Kaius, and me. We all had the same mindset and energy going into this trip, which made it very enjoyable.

After landing in Queenstown we picked up the rental car, did some shopping at Warehouse (an NZ department store), and got Domino's Pizza for lunch. Then, we headed towards Te Anau, which is the last town on the way to the Fiordland National Park. The drive there already gave us a good impression of the beautiful nature that we would see in the next days, with vast meadows and mountains surrounding us on all sides. We arrived at the campground where we had rented a small hut for two nights, unpacked our things and went to a nearby lake to watch the sunset. It was later that evening when we started our daily ritual of making pasta for dinner, before going to bed early to be ready for the next day.

The next day we woke up early before sunrise, at which time it was still unbelievably cold (just above zero degrees C), and the freezing cold water in the campground toilets did not make things better. We packed as many layers of clothing as we possibly could and made the drive up to Milford Sound, which is the most famous fiord in New Zealand. Of course, we made a few stops on the way to admire the landscape during the early morning hours.

In Milford Sound, we took a boat tour through the fiord, which went all the way to the ocean. On it, we learned all about fiords and this one in particular. A fiord is a canyon that was carved by a glacier, which is then flooded by the ocean. I still find it hard to believe that a glacier can carve into anything, but apparently the immense weight of the ice, and the constant movement created by the ever-increasing weight getting pushed down from the top over thousands of years does a number on some rocks. And now, we get to marvel at the beauty it has created.

After a brief morning session of stretching and yoga at the roadside, we drove back to Queenstown to do some shopping, which concluded with us buying 5kg of pasta, 10 glasses of sauce and breakfast for 3 days. Afterwards, we found ourselves back at Domino's Pizza, because who can resist a pizza for 6 NZD? (That is 3€). With the food sorted out, we drove to our next destination, Wanaka, where we went to a lavender farm, and then despite running out of daylight, did a hike up *Rocky Peak*. The hike turned out to be a bit tricky, due to some muddy steep sections. Not keen on descending those in the dark, Jessica and Vincent turned around, and the rest of us continued to the top, where we were rewarded with a stunning sunset scenery of the valley. With only one working headlamp (mine was safely at home in Brisbane, grrrr), we still made it down safely, as the moon was bright enough.

Following the most beautiful evening was was probably my favorite morning of the trip. The autumn colors were immaculate, and we saw a shepherd and his dogs herding sheep across the road in front of us. We stopped at the blue pools, and several water falls, before making our way to the west coast, where we headed to Fox Glacier. Fox Glacier is not only the name of the town where our next Hostel was, but also the name of the glacier that we went to see. It is one of only two glaciers that penetrate into the rainforest. We then went to a viewpoint at a lake, called the *View of Views*, where usually you can see a nice reflection of Mount Cook and Mount Tasman in the lake, but we were unlucky with the clouds hiding the peaks and the ducks ducking up the water. Mount Cook is the highest mountain in New Zealand, peaking at 3724m. In a few days we will get to see it again, after having traversed the mountain range to access it from the other side.
